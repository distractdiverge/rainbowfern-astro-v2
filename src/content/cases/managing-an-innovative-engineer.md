## Context

I managed the backend web services team at Swift Financial. One of my engineers was deeply passionate about starting new projects. He wanted to solve problems the business hadn't yet named as problems.

That energy was valuable, and it was also pulling against the team's committed work and causing friction with other teams.

## Constraint

- The team was a **hub** with many internal customers. Committed work had to ship.
- **Shutting him down** would have cost the team its most inventive engineer.
- **Letting him run free** would have meant missed deadlines and more clashes.

## What I did

- **Met with him often, one on one,** and connected him with engineers on other teams who were facing related problems.
- **Ran the team as a hub.** I collected what other teams were working on and brought it back as "problems that need solving." I defined the problem and the outcome we needed, and left the approach to him.
- **Made an explicit agreement:** he could pursue his own architectural ideas once the high-priority work was finished.
- **Asked him to share what he built.** Once a new system was working, he presented it to our team and to other departments, along with how it could help them.
- **Built sharing into the team's habits.** Monthly developer lunch-and-learns, and anyone back from a conference wrote up and presented what they'd learned.

**My part vs. the team's:** the structure and the agreement were mine. The ideas and the building were his.

## Outcome

- He took ownership of a product he cared about, and the core work still shipped.
- Presenting his work turned clashes into recognition from other teams.
- He became one of the strongest engineers on the team, and was later promoted to manager.
- The team kept working well after I moved on, through the PayPal acquisition and several years of reorganizations.

## Why it matters to a client

Some of the best engineers don't fit the usual shape. Many of them are neurodivergent, and many have been told their way of working is a problem.

If you're managing someone like that, or you *are* someone like that, I can help design an arrangement that keeps the drive, gives it clear edges, and makes the work visible. This is the heart of my mentoring and coaching.
