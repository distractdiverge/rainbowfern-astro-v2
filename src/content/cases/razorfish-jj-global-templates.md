## Context

At Razorfish, I led development of a global template system for Johnson & Johnson, built on Drupal. The goal was to standardize how content was formatted across J&J's sites around the world.

## Constraint

- **It had to be extremely flexible.** Each region had different content needs and expectations.
- **20+ languages,** including right-to-left scripts like Hebrew and Arabic and character sets like Chinese and Japanese.
- **One codebase.** A page in the US and a page in Saudi Arabia had to use the same HTML and CSS, with only the content changing.

## What I did

- **Worked closely with UX and design.** I turned large UX diagrams and component plans into something that would work technically.
- **Parameterized the HTML and CSS** (using Sass) so layouts could flip between left-to-right and right-to-left, and handle different character sets, without separate code.
- **Interviewed J&J stakeholders and content creators** to learn what they actually needed to do.
- **Fed technical insight back into the UX work.** My role grew from front-end engineer into something closer to a UX partner.
- Ran the work in regular sprints and coordinated a team across Europe and the US.

**My part vs. the team's:** I led the technical design and the stakeholder interviews. UX and design owned the experience; developers in Europe and the US built alongside me.

## Outcome

- The template system shipped and was a clear success for the client.
- Regions could publish in their own language and reading direction from one shared codebase.
- I learned how much page structure changes from culture to culture. It's still one of my favorite projects.

## Why it matters to a client

Accessibility and internationalization go best when engineering and design solve them together, early.

If you need one system to serve very different audiences, or your designers and developers keep talking past each other, I can sit between them, ask the people who'll use it what they need, and turn that into something maintainable.
