## Context

PayPal Credit's engineering organization had hundreds of engineers, possibly close to a thousand. As an architect, I didn't manage one team. My job was to keep integration work lined up across many of them.

The work spanned Baltimore, Arizona (at least 50 engineers) and a new team in India, plus security engineering. I worked from Pennsylvania.

## Constraint

- **No direct authority.** Most of the people I coordinated didn't report to me.
- **Time zones and distance.** Three main locations, and a team in India that didn't exist yet.
- **Security had to be part of it,** not an afterthought.
- Systems from the Swift Financial acquisition needed **new people to maintain them**.

## What I did

- **Ran a daily sync** across Baltimore, Arizona and my location. It was short on purpose: align, then let each team go do its work.
- **Called extra coordination meetings** only when something needed them.
- **Kept security engineering in the loop** so integration decisions didn't surprise them later.
- **Helped hire the India team.** I interviewed the first manager, then interviewed that manager's direct reports, and co-led interviews for other engineering candidates.
- **Presented the architecture** of systems I'd built to more than 300 engineers in India, which helped attract people to the team.

**My part vs. the team's:** I ran the coordination rhythm and took part in hiring. The India manager built and led the team day to day.

## Outcome

- Integration work moved forward across three locations without a single team becoming the bottleneck.
- The India team I directly supported grew to about 5–6 engineers plus a lead, and some hires came out of the presentation. A few later moved on, as people do.
- Security was a participant throughout, not a gate at the end.

## Why it matters to a client

Coordination is a skill, not a side effect of seniority. It's mostly small, steady habits: a short daily sync, clear handoffs, and the right people in the conversation early.

If your teams are spread out, or you're about to hire people to look after systems they didn't build, I can help set up the rhythm and the documentation they'll need.
